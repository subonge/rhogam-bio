I honestly can't tell where the week went.

It feels like the exam was weeks ago,
and like I've been wearing the watch for weeks too.

But both of those happened this week!
I only realized it while sorting through photos and thinking about what to write for week 9.

I guess that's a sign of how dense this stretch of time has been?

## Day 1 — Sep 28 (Mon) · Goodbye, Proteins!

Today was finally the last day of Proteins.
If you look back at last week's post, you can see how confident this guy was,
and this was an exam whose shocking difficulty left me deeply crushed..

Luckily, it didn't seem like I was the only one who found it hard.
Sighs were coming from every direction.. and afterward we all gathered and gave the professor a good piece of our minds.

The donuts set out in the student lounge calmed my angry heart a bit,
and then, completely out of the blue, my Big sib had prepared a Starbucks gift card and a cake that looked like candy, and my mood was totally restored!

Here's hoping Genes goes a lot better..

## Day 5 - Oct 2 (Fri) · Experiments or Rest, What Really Matters?

Starting today, for about four days, so until next Monday, my supervisor is away from the lab.

There's an event called a Gala, where wealthy people are invited, research is presented, and donations are raised.
(This was a new culture to me too.. I wonder if there's something like this in Korea?)
It's being held in LA, so today and next Monday I get some time to myself.

Still, we'd talked through what I should work on while they're away,
and I got started on experiments right away.
That said, I'd designed the experiment with much trickier details than usual, so it was a day that demanded a bit more focus.

I also want to write down a conversation I had with another postdoc that stuck with me.

Every time I happened to come in on a weekend, he was always there, so I was curious.
Do you like coming in on weekends?

He looked at me like I was strange.. who would like that, he said.
He told me that back when he was a PhD student, he never came in on weekends.

He said a PhD is very long and hard, and that's exactly why keeping a good work-life balance matters even more.
And he told me that what's important is to focus as hard as you can during the time you're at school, design your experiments very efficiently, and get results.

The biggest strength of this lab is that there are great postdocs and great colleagues like this everywhere you turn.

...

Anyway, that's the story of how I squeezed in lunch in the middle of experiments, spent a moment debating whether to buy a Stanmore III during Amazon Prime Day on October 6 and 7 next week, finished all my experiments, went for a short workout during the hour and a half the measuring instrument was running, and grilled thin-sliced pork belly for dinner.

I usually work out at a place called the MD Student Center, and the lobby was decorated so nicely that it put me in a good mood too. And the combination of my new lift-top table and the TV is a work of art.

## Day 6 - Oct 3 (Sat) · Am I Finally Creditworthy?!

The day has finally come.
The last gate of paperwork hell: a credit card.

How long I've waited for this day.
It's been almost a month since I applied for my SSN,
and it's already been more than two months since I came to the US.

Chase offers many different credit cards,
but the ones an outsider like me (with zero credit) can get are very limited.
And my friends, who got their SSNs well before me, were issued the most basic credit card, the Chase Freedom Rise.

At the time I was impatient, so I was planning to get the same card.
But the more urgent things are, the more you should take the long way around.
Maybe, since more time had passed, I'd built up enough history to get a card one level higher?
With that thought, I applied for the Unlimited, a step above the Freedom Rise.

The result: a huge success!
The card was approved the moment I applied, and now I'm officially creditworthy too!
I'll play the Credit Game hard with this and build good credit.

And by the time I pass my Qualifying exam two years from now, the gate to becoming a PhD candidate,
will my good credit let me splurge on a Tesla Model Y on installments? (Of course, I'm just thinking about it)

I looked into ways to buy it right away, and Amazon shipping was taking almost two weeks.
Luckily, the Target nearby offered online order and pickup, so I went for it immediately.

But my wrist turned out to be much thinner than I thought, and it didn't fit.
On top of that, there were lots of reviews saying the Fitbit Air's measurements are somewhat inaccurate,

## Day 7 - Oct 4 (Sun) · Katy Trail Run

Maybe because it rained all week, the Texas heat has eased off a notch.

The weather was cool, and a refreshing breeze was blowing.
I was a little worried the wet ground wouldn't be good for running, but I decided to go out and run anyway.

I finally went to the Katy Trail, which I'd always put off, blaming the weather, telling myself I'd go someday.
The scenery was so much better than I expected that I couldn't focus on running and ended up sightseeing instead.

I ran for about 20 minutes and walked for the other 40 or so, sorting through all kinds of stray thoughts,
and breathing in all that fresh forest air while my head got a reset made it the best kind of rest.
I'll have to make chances to run more often.

Week 9 in the US.
In a way it was a very smooth week, but there was one big problem.
On Tuesday, September 30, I went to the SSA office to get my SSN card reissued.

Here's what happened:
the receipt said that if you don't receive your SSN card by mail within two weeks of applying,
you have to apply again,

and since I'd waited a whole four weeks and it still hadn't come,
I went to apply again.

But oh no! The very next day after reapplying, Wednesday, October 1, it arrived in the mail.
If only I'd waited one more day.. but what's done is done.

At that point, I thought I had to keep waiting for the newly requested SSN card, but it turned out that wasn't the case.
Since the new card is issued with the same number anyway, the SSN on the earlier letter is valid too! (I only found that out on Saturday, right before applying for the credit card..)

Anyway, it all worked out in the end,
but it was a brutal critical hit of the bitter taste of American bureaucracy, and I wasn't in a great mood about it.
Even so, it's a touching ending where I successfully got my credit card and everything got resolved.

The US seems to be a place that demands a little more patience than I actually have.
