Week 15.

I took my exam,
and it was a week where all the plans I'd lined up had just been waiting for the exam to end.

I visited the home of a friend who lives in the US for the first time,
and tried an American sauna — a special week.
(I won't pretend I didn't miss Korean jjimjilbangs.)

## Day 1 — Nov 09 (Mon) · Second Genes Exam

It was the day of my fourth exam, one I thought I'd gotten used to.

Guess I was wrong about being used to it.
The week right after an exam still drains me.

Still, to celebrate finishing, I happily munched on the snacks they'd set out,
and had some tasty Chinese-style chicken for lunch.

I should have picked fried rice, but "white rice" was the only thing I caught, so I chose it —
and ended up with a total disaster: plain rice. Listening matters.

Then I headed to the lab to do my actual job.
From my very first day in the lab, I learned to label every agent one by one with the label maker,
and I think that's turned out to be a really good habit.

You can print labels in all kinds of formats as needed, from Eppendorf tubes to bulky agents.
As the materials I use in experiments keep piling up, I think I'll need not just accurate labeling but also an accurate map of where each material is. So I'm building a new tool for that mapping, and so far it seems to be working well.

And tomorrow, I'm inviting friends over to make tteokbokki together and play board games! Our apartment complex has (somewhat dirty-looking, but still) outdoor grills where you can barbecue, so I'd like to try using them sometime.

Oh, and after waiting and waiting and waiting!! I finally got my Texas driver's license.

I'm now an honorary Texan.

## Day 2 — Nov 10 (Tue) · Tteokbokki and Board Games

Today was the HHMI Luncheon.

HHMI (Howard Hughes Medical Institute) is one of the most prestigious private biomedical research organizations in the US. If you're selected, you get funding for seven years, with a lot of freedom in how you spend it.

It's a basic biomedical research organization founded by Howard Hughes, an incredibly famous billionaire.
(He's the subject of The Aviator, a movie about aviation.)

Anyway, since Dr. Casanova, whose lab I'm rotating in, is an HHMI Investigator, I had the chance to attend the luncheon as a member of the lab.
(Honestly, I just went for the food.)

The food was pretty good, and with live music plus a hand sanitizer with "HHMI" printed on it, it was a pleasant meal.

...

And then, dinner!
Today we'd decided to make tteokbokki together.
(Well, really just buying a ready-made kit and cooking it.)

Everyone kept putting it off, saying their own places weren't ready yet,
so we had no choice but to end up at my place again.

We bought gimbap, sundae, and dakgangjeong and cooked them along with a Yupdduk tteokbokki kit,
and it was so, so delicious..
(I'm really glad I have Korean friends here.)

Then we played a board game called Wingspan.
It's an engine-building game where you collect resources, spend them to play birds onto your field, and then use the birds' abilities to get more resources and score points.

I studied hard on YouTube, but explaining it to someone else was a whole different level.
If I'd explained it in a more fun way, would my friends have enjoyed playing it more?

The game is a bit long, so by the end everyone kind of wanted to go home (ㅠ)
Next time I should start with a game that's easier to get into. I still have a mountain of board games to play.

Today was just an ordinary day like any other.

The good news is that a sample I'm studying gave an interesting result,
so I've been running a chain of related experiments, and I'm making real progress.

But impatience ruins everything, so as the results get closer, I'm holding back that growing urge
and decided to look at my research from a step back.

Anyway, Vu, a postdoc I'm working with, recommended a YouTube channel

Maybe because part of me still wants to play a little more (?), I'm focusing on resting properly after work and on weekends, without thinking about research.

If I keep living like this, won't the center of my life naturally shift toward research?

## Day 4 - Nov 12 (Thu) · Costco!

Today was the New Faculty Research Forum.
It ran from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., nearly half a day,
with newly hired faculty members each giving a short introduction to their research.

Helen Hobbs, who opened the event, is an amazing researcher who discovered PCSK9 through human genetics, leading to the development of PCSK9 inhibitors.

She found that people carrying loss-of-function variants in PCSK9 had low levels of LDL, the "bad" cholesterol, and that discovery was developed into a treatment for cardiovascular disease.

Watching a researcher who has fully become exactly the persona I'm aiming for — starting from a basic science discovery and carrying it all the way to a therapy — filled me with awe.

...

And in the evening, I unexpectedly ended up tagging along to Costco.

Here's why.

Maybe because the temperature suddenly dropped today, my tire pressure warning light came on.
I was wondering where to put air in, and my Korean friends said they always do it at Costco and offered to come along and show me.

Honestly, it's pretty far, and there wasn't really anything I needed to buy, but I'd never been to one in the US, so I went along out of curiosity.

Leaving my friends to sweep up armfuls of stuff,
I exercised my very best self-control for the sake of my aching wallet.

But I couldn't resist the rotisserie chicken..
This much, at this quality, for $5 — isn't that insane?
(The amount in the photo is only about 1/5 of it..)

Overall it was so cheap and good
that I talked with another Korean friend who'd come along about getting a membership together.

## Day 5 - Nov 13 (Fri) · Korean BBQ!

Today was the day I met my girlfriend's father, who was in the US on a business trip from Korea.

That first sentence may feel a bit abrupt, but that's just how it was.

Since I hadn't gotten him a gift yet, and I also needed wine as a gift for my cousin's family, whom I'm visiting in DC, I rushed off to Total Wine.

I picked a William Chris Mourvèdre, from the first winery in Texas to be named one of the world's 50 best.
Honestly, I know nothing about wine, so a friend who enjoys wine and came with me picked it out.

And since I was out anyway — In-N-Out! It's always so good.
And if you can, it's better to eat in. The fries are so good, and there are jalapeño pickles you can only get inside the restaurant, which are the real kicker.

While I was out, I got a coffee transfusion and finally visited a board game store I'd been putting off.
I bought a game called The Crew, and since I needed sleeves for my Dominion cards, I bought about 300 of those too.

I also have great memories of playing a game called Ra and wanted to buy it,
but the regular edition was sold out, and only the Pharaoh edition was left.
(The tokens are fancier, and it costs about 2.5x as much.)

So I found that one online and ordered it too.
The board games keep piling up — this is a problem.

After work, I had dinner with my girlfriend's father and his friend, just the three of us.
The Korean BBQ place we went to, my first in a while, was really delicious, but I was pretty nervous, so I'm a bit sad I couldn't fully taste it.

Still, I was so relieved that he liked the wine I gave him!

## Day 6 - Nov 14 (Sat) · LoL Worlds GenG vs BLG

Today was the Worlds final.
The tournament ran through the semifinals in Dallas, Texas, and the final is held in New York.

A friend in my cohort who loves LoL said he was having everyone over to watch the match at his place, and invited me.
Going alone felt a little awkward, so I went over with a Korean friend.

It's Rex, the friend I went to a basketball game with before;
his parents are from the Middle East, and he was born in the US.

His house had not one but two cats (everyone has a cat except me),
and a home gym too, so it was fun to look around.

We had a great spread of snacks: nachos and chips, sandwiches his mom had made,
and pizza my friend ordered.

Then the Worlds final began, and the result was a 1:3 loss for GenG.
It's a shame the Korean team lost, but with China's LPL league having looked almost dead, a Chinese team beating Korean teams four times in a row and lifting the trophy felt like a sign of the Chinese league's revival.

I found myself hoping that good players and good teams keep coming from other countries too, so friendly competition makes the whole scene bigger.

There I also met Sihan, a Chinese friend from my cohort, and we chatted —
I had no idea, but it turns out he lives in my apartment complex, in the unit diagonally across from mine!
We got pretty close and had a great talk, and promised to hang out together sometime.

## Day 7 - Nov 15 (Sun) · WorldSprings

Today was the day I went to a sauna called WorldSprings.

Writing all this out, I see why this week felt so unusually long..
I think I packed in way too many plans.

But this week was my only chance to have fun after the exam..
Because the Monday after next, my first Cells exam is already scheduled.

Anyway, I finally got to go to the sauna I'd promised to visit with a Japanese friend for a while.

But.. around Thursday, I heard it would rain that day and wondered whether to cancel,
and my tough-guy Japanese friend said he doesn't care about the weather and asked what I thought,
so I trusted his steadiness and went along — and we ended up at an outdoor hot spring in gloomy weather.

The hot spring we arrived at was better than I imagined!
It's normally around $100, but we got in for about $50 each thanks to a promotion, which was great to begin with.
And the facilities were so clean and modern — especially for the US.

I got to experience something called an Aufguss sauna, imported from Norway:
in a space that holds about 60–70 people, you enjoy the sauna for about 10 minutes with music, breathing in scents like ginger and orange. It was a really fun experience.

Right after that came the cold plunge — jumping straight into cold water for the ultimate hot-and-cold contrast. I'd heard the proper way is to go back and forth between the cold water and the sauna several times, but it was pretty cold outside, so I couldn't.

Still, after the sauna my head felt much clearer.
I stopped by H-mart for groceries, got a haircut, and wrapped up a long week.
