A few months after moving to the US, once you've gotten used to your first credit card, the next question comes naturally.

> "I'm flying to Korea once a year anyway — couldn't I save at least a little on that ticket with a credit card?"

In the US, this is commonly called the **miles game**: you earn points or miles through card spending, then turn them into flights.

This post is my attempt to work through that question for my own situation. Bottom line first: **if you're not set on Korean Air, the first travel card should be the Chase Sapphire Preferred**; **if you're aiming for Korean Air miles, all the way up to business class, it's the SKYPASS card**. And either way, it makes sense to apply **after building up a bit more credit history**.

::: {.callout-note}
Card offers and benefits are **as of November 2026** and change often. Always check the latest terms on the issuer's official page before applying. This is just a personal record, not financial advice.
:::

## My situation (the assumptions behind the numbers)

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Home | Dallas (DFW airport) |
| Korea round trip | Once a year, $1,500–2,000 on Korean Air |
| Monthly card spending | $1,000–1,500 |
| Current card | Chase Freedom Unlimited (opened October 2026, my first US card) |

I used the midpoints: **$1,250 a month, $15,000 a year** (including a $1,750 flight, and $3,000 of that on dining).

---

## Thing to know first #1 — Korean Air doesn't fly US domestic routes

Korean Air has plenty of **Incheon ↔ US** routes, but **it doesn't operate domestic flights between US cities itself**.

- Dallas (DFW) ↔ Incheon has a **Korean Air nonstop**.
- For domestic trips like Dallas → Atlanta, you'll need to fly **Delta** or another airline.
- Korean Air and Delta are partners, so you can combine a domestic leg and an international leg on one itinerary, or earn SKYPASS miles on Delta flights. Earning rates depend on the fare class, though.
- For reference, DFW is an **American Airlines hub**. If you travel around the US a lot, Korean Air miles alone will only take you so far.

---

## Thing to know first #2 — Chase points can't go to Korean Air

The core of the miles game is **transferring** points. Chase points transfer 1:1 to partners like United, Air Canada, Flying Blue (Air France–KLM), and Hyatt.

But **Korean Air SKYPASS was dropped as a Chase transfer partner in 2018.** These days, there are essentially two ways to earn Korean Air miles:

1. Earn directly with a Korean Air co-branded card (SKYPASS Visa)
2. Earn by flying Korean Air, Delta, and other partner airlines

So this comparison is really **"Korean Air miles vs. Chase points you can send to many different airlines."**

| Dining | 3x | 3x | 2 miles |
| Travel | 5x via Chase Travel | 5x via Chase Travel, 2x other travel | 2 miles on Korean Air tickets & hotels |
| Other perks | — | $100 annual Chase Travel hotel credit, no foreign transaction fees | 5% off a Korean Air ticket once a year, 2 lounge vouchers, no foreign transaction fees |

There's one point here that's easy to miss.

**On its own, Freedom Unlimited is a cash-back card — but if you also have the Sapphire Preferred, you can combine its points into Sapphire and transfer them to airlines.** In other words, the moment you add Sapphire, Freedom's 1.5% turns into "1.5x transferable points." That's what makes the Chase combo so strong.

## Running the numbers

I valued points and miles at **1.3 cents each**. The real value can be 1 cent or 2+ cents depending on which flights you book and how.

Here's how spending is split in each setup:

- **Freedom only**: everything on Freedom (cash back)
- **Freedom + Sapphire**: dining and flights on Sapphire, everything else on Freedom (points combined and transferred)
- **Freedom + SKYPASS**: dining and Korean Air flights on SKYPASS, everything else on Freedom (cash back)

### Value every year (from year 2)

| | Freedom only | Freedom + Sapphire | Freedom + SKYPASS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everyday spending $10,250 | $154 | 15,375 pts | $154 |
| Dining $3,000 | $90 | 9,000 pts | 6,000 miles |
| Korean Air flight $1,750 | $26 | 3,500 pts | 3,500 miles |
| Points/miles value (1.3¢) | — | $362 | $124 |
| 5% ticket discount (base fare only) | — | — | about $70 |
| Hotel credit | — | $100 (if used) | — |
| Annual fee | $0 | −$95 | −$99 |
| **Net yearly value** | **$270** | **about $367** ($267 without the credit) | **about $249** |

### First year (including the welcome bonus)

| | Freedom only | Freedom + Sapphire | Freedom + SKYPASS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome bonus value (1.3¢) | — | $975 | $520 |
| **First-year total** | **$270** | **about $1,342** | **about $769** |

To sum up:

- **In year one, the bonus is everything.** The difference in yearly earning is tens to a hundred dollars; the bonus is worth hundreds.
- **From year two, SKYPASS comes out slightly below Freedom on its own.** It only earns its annual fee if you redeem miles for well above 1.3 cents — business-class award tickets being the classic example.
- **Sapphire's year-two value depends on whether you actually use the $100 hotel credit.**

## Can my spending meet the bonus requirements?

| | Requirement | My expected 3-month spending |
|---|---|---|
| Sapphire Preferred | $5,000 in 3 months | about $3,000–4,500 |
| SKYPASS Signature | $4,000 in 90 days | about $3,000–4,500 |

Everyday spending alone makes it tight. So **if you open the card 1–3 months before paying for your Korea round trip ($1,500–2,000)**, money you'd be spending anyway naturally gets you there.

Inflating your spending just for a bonus defeats the whole purpose.

---

## Things to check before applying

**1. Length of credit history**
My first card was opened in October 2026, so by next March I'll have about 5 months of history, and 6 months by April. Chase tends to be conservative with applicants who have short histories, so if there's no rush, it's safer to build 6 months to a year first. Of course, time alone doesn't guarantee approval.

**2. SKYPASS card residency requirement**
U.S. Bank says SKYPASS cards are issued only to US residents. How that applies to students on a visa (F-1, etc.) is worth confirming directly with U.S. Bank before you apply.

**3. Where you can use the lounge vouchers**
SKYPASS lounge vouchers are for Korean Air lounges, so don't count on them for flights out of DFW. It's more realistic to plan on using them when flying back out of Incheon. Double-check the eligible lounges in the card terms.

**4. Payment habits**
The miles game assumes you **pay your balance in full every month**. A single interest charge can wipe out a whole year of rewards.

## So here's my plan

- **For now**: build credit history with Freedom Unlimited alone. Keep the balance reported on the statement closing date low relative to my limit, and pay in full every month.
- **From April 2027**: check for preapproval offers and start thinking about a first travel card, timing the application around paying for my next flight to Korea.
- **I'm leaning toward the Sapphire Preferred as my first travel card.** The bonus is bigger, and with Freedom's points pooled in, I can use them flexibly on airlines and hotels beyond Korean Air.
- **I'll add SKYPASS if I set a goal of flying Korean Air business class on miles.** Whether to carry two annual-fee cards is something I'll decide after seeing how I actually used them in the first year.

In the end, it all comes back to one question.

> **Do I really have to fly Korean Air to get to Korea?**

If other airlines are fine as long as the price is right → the Chase combo.
If Korean Air (especially business class) is the goal → SKYPASS.

My answer is closer to the first one for now. When I book my next flight to Korea, I'll follow up with how it actually turned out.
